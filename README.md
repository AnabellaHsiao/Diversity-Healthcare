A unified, accessible digital healthcare platform designed to bridge the gap between patients and clinicians by eliminating data fragmentation, improving accessibility, and streamlining cross-platform medical record sharing.

## 🎯 Problem Statement

Modern healthcare digital infrastructure suffers from extreme fragmentation across clinical platforms, leading to severe operational and diagnostic bottlenecks:

* **Inefficiency:** Severe delays in diagnosis and treatment delivery caused by scattered medical records.
* **Ineffectiveness:** High administrative burdens when manually gathering patient data across disparate systems, hindering clinical research and patient care.
* **Inequity:** Low-income households and rural populations face disproportionate rates of misdiagnosis due to limited resources and complex record-fetching requirements across regional platforms.

---

## 💡 Solution Overview & Requirements

The **Diversity Healthcare App** unifies patient health management through tailored mobile and web interfaces built with strict accessibility standards and regional integration.

### Target Audience
* **Patients:** Elders, parents/guardians booking for minors, individuals with disabilities, and general adults.
* **Clinicians:** Doctors, nurses, and hospital/clinic staff.

### Key System Requirements
* **Mobile Patient App:** Simplified mobile dashboard for viewing active diagnoses, prescriptions, and appointment schedules.
* **Web Desktop Portal:** High-functionality desktop interface for clinicians and patients to input, review, and modify medical records.
* **Accessibility-First Design:** Enhanced UI elements tailored for users with disabilities, including high color contrast modes, size-adjustable text/buttons, and descriptive image alt-text.
* **Regional Data Integration:** Native connectivity for provincial/state health cards (e.g., OHIP) and private insurance statuses to pre-load health history before visits.

## 📚 Literature Review Summary

### Problem Context
* **State Vaccine Portal Usability:** Howe et al. (2021) showed that 14% of US state vaccine sites lacked mobile responsiveness, and 92% forced manual location searching, demonstrating widespread public health software fragmentation.
* **EHR Implementation Barriers:** Muli et al. (2025) identified complex documentation templates, high clinician workload, and digital literacy barriers as key factors hindering electronic health record adoption.
* **Inter-Specialty Collaboration:** Vos et al. (2020) demonstrated that rigid authorization boundaries and specialty-isolated interfaces restrict cross-discipline clinical coordination.

### Solution Context
* **Accessibility Deficits:** Jones et al. (2018) highlighted that over 80% of US residents with disabilities live with chronic illness, yet only 17% use digital health apps due to poor accessibility design.
* **Patient Outcome Improvements:** Systematic reviews by Tapuria et al. (2021) and Brands et al. (2022) revealed that granting direct patient access to clear, unified health records directly improves medication adherence, disease knowledge, and chronic condition management.
